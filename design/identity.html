your own

Draw two sentences. Get a font of your own handwriting, and keep it.

This page sets out the look and feel: a sheet of paper, three inks, one pen and a small red press. Everything that looks handwritten here was drawn stroke by stroke with the same pen the app will use, and the pen further down really works.

Tap the logo to watch it write itself again.

The idea

A type foundry the size of a sheet of paper.

The app should feel like a friendly desk object: warm paper, good ink, something that goes thunk. The brand stays quiet so the person's own handwriting is the loudest thing on screen.

Only ink is handwritten

No script fonts, ever. Every handwritten mark came from a pen: ours on the brand, then yours in the product.

Paper, not screen

Surfaces are sheets: nearly square corners, soft stacked shadows, light from above. Lots of white, so there's room to write.

Things have weight

Letters spring, sheets settle, the press lands with one heavy pull. Nothing fades in from nowhere.

You are the typeface

One sans, three inks, no decoration. Once your font exists, the site starts using it, down to the icon.

Mark

The logo is half typed, half written.

“your own” is set in Figtree. “font” is drawn on the writing guides the app gives you: a solid baseline and a dashed x-height. The guides show while it writes, then step away.

The drawn half follows real type metrics, the ones every glyph in your font will sit on. After your font is built, the logo on your screen is redrawn in your hand.

The app icon is one letter on a ruled tile, and the letter changes. Paper tiles are the default; the solid ink tiles are for share cards and stickers.

Colour

One sheet of paper, three inks.

The accent colours are the pen's three inks, so the brand palette and the product's choices are the same thing. Everything else is paper.

The press

Your letters, cast and pressed.

The build animation is a little screw press: red enamel, two blue knobs, one heavy pull. More desk toy than museum piece. Each step runs while the real pipeline does that job, so it never pretends.

  1. Lift

    Your letters peel off the page.

    strokes → outlines

  2. Cast

    Each one hardens into a piece of type.

    union + smoothing

  3. Set

    They line up in a row, spaced by eye.

    metrics

  4. Ink

    A roller passes over, in your ink.

    variants + build

  5. Press

    One pull, and the sheet comes out written in your font.

    opentype.js → .otf

Type is cast backwards

So the press can print it the right way round. These three read “you” on paper. Each piece is standard type height, 0.918 inch, because the details should be true even when nobody checks.

Motion

Three springs, and nothing else.

No easing curves, no fades from nowhere. Every movement in the app uses one of three Motion springs, chosen by how heavy the thing is.

Nib

Ink filling, taps, buttons. Quick, with a small bounce.

stiffness 520 · damping 32 · mass 1

Paper

Sheets, words flying to the tray, cards. Floaty, settles softly.

stiffness 140 · damping 14 · mass 1

Press

The press, and only the press. Heavy, one clear thunk.

stiffness 260 · damping 30 · mass 3
With reduced motion on, everything jumps to its end state and the press becomes a single fade.
